FAQs

Frequently asked questions.

DED.

What is DED.?

DED. is a death planning platform. It walks you through everything from your funeral playlist to your passwords, so the people you leave behind aren't left guessing.

Think of it as the instructions you'd want to leave behind, all in one place.

Can I sign up now?

Not quite. DED. is in private beta and accounts are invite-only while we test and improve it. Join the waitlist and we'll invite you when a spot opens.

How much is DED.?

We're in beta right now. Join the waitlist and you'll be first to hear about access and pricing.

Is DED. secure?

Your plan holds some of the most personal stuff you own, so we treat it that way. Only you, and the people you choose, can see your plan.

Bank and payment details are never shown to your DED. team. If you save a phone or computer passcode, it's locked on your device before it's sent, and once it's saved nobody can view it, not your team and not even you.

Who can see my plan?

Only you, until you decide otherwise. You choose your DED. team, the people who'll carry out your wishes, and you can invite them, share with them or take access away at any time.

What happens to my plan when I die?

Your DED. team already has what you've chosen to share with them: your run sheet, your checklist and everything you've left them, ready to use.

Your legacy contact lets us know, we check with you first, and then your private things go only to the people you chose. See How DED. works below.

Is DED. a legal will?

No. DED. sits alongside your will, not instead of it. Your will says who gets what; DED. covers everything else.

If you don't have a will yet, talk to a lawyer or a trustee service.

Can I change my mind?

Constantly, if you want. Your plan updates whenever you do. Change the song, swap the outfit, uninvite your cousin.

I'm not dying. Why would I do this now?

Because nobody plans to die suddenly. Sorting it now means your people get to grieve instead of guessing.

How DED. works

How does DED. work?

1. Make your plan. We ask the questions, from your funeral and your final fit to your documents, accounts and loose ends. Go at your own pace and change it whenever you like.

2. Pick your people. Invite your DED. team to see your plan now, so nobody's left guessing. You can also choose a legacy contact and a backup for after you die.

3. When you die. Your legacy contact lets us know. We check with you first, just in case. Then your private things go only to the people you chose.

Nothing is released until you've had the chance to say no.

Why use DED.?

When someone dies, the people left behind have to make a lot of decisions fast, usually while they're grieving and guessing what you'd have wanted.

DED. puts your wishes, your important info and your loose ends in one place, and shares them with the people who'll need them. They get to focus on saying goodbye instead of hunting for passwords or arguing about the playlist.

What's a legacy contact?

The person who lets us know you've died, and who receives the things you've locked away for after, like your phone or computer passcode. You also pick a backup, who steps in if they can't and can flag anything that doesn't look right.

We check their ID and you confirm it's really them, all while you're still around. They don't need their own DED. account.

How long will my loved ones have to wait?

There's a short waiting period after your death is reported, so you have the chance to say no if it's a mistake. Uploading a death certificate can make it shorter.

Anything they need straight away, like your funeral wishes, is already in your shared plan, so nobody has to wait to plan the funeral.

What if someone reports my death by mistake?

We email you straight away, and you cancel it just by signing in. Your backup is told too and can flag it. Nothing is released in the meantime.

Do you store my passwords?

Not your everyday passwords. If Chrome already saves them, you don't need to change anything. Your legacy contact can get to them through your laptop or phone, so you can save that one passcode in DED., locked so nobody can view it.

Prefer a password manager? Google, Apple, Bitwarden, 1Password and Keeper all have ways to pass things on to someone you trust.
